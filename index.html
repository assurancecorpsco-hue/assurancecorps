UAE Corporate Tax Audit Readiness: Records, Reconciliations and FTA Review Checklist for Dubai Businesses

Dubai businesses must prepare more than a Corporate Tax return for an FTA review. They must show where their reported figures came from. Each important amount should match accounting records and supporting documents. Companies must also explain tax adjustments, deductions, exemptions, and related party transactions.

UAE Corporate Tax law generally requires businesses to keep relevant records for seven years. The period starts after the end of the relevant tax period.

Good audit readiness depends on three things: complete records, accurate reconciliations, and supported tax positions. These help businesses find errors before they become bigger problems.

Use the Corporate Tax Audit Readiness Checklist to assess your company's preparation.

How Can a Dubai Business Determine Whether It Is Ready for an FTA Corporate Tax Review?

A Dubai business should first check whether its tax figures have clear evidence. The finance team should compare accounting records, tax calculations, and filed returns. Every important difference needs an explanation and supporting documents.

This review helps managers identify missing records and possible tax risks.

The five questions every finance manager should answer before an FTA review

A finance manager should answer these five questions before confirming audit readiness.

  • Can every material Corporate Tax return amount be traced to the general ledger?
  • Do the trial balance, financial statements, and tax computation match?
  • Can the company support its deductions, exemptions, and tax adjustments?
  • Are related party and free zone transactions properly documented?
  • Can staff retrieve requested records quickly and accurately?

Each answer should have supporting evidence. A simple yes or no cannot prove compliance.

For example, a company may report AED 2 million in sales. Its finance manager should trace that amount through the sales ledger. The records should also explain credit notes, adjustments, and other differences.

How to classify readiness gaps as critical, significant or administrative

Not every missing document creates the same level of risk. Companies should classify problems based on possible tax exposure and urgency.

Risk level Example Recommended action
Critical Filed taxable income cannot be supported Start an immediate technical review
Significant Important expense invoices are missing Retrieve evidence and assess tax treatment
Administrative A document has an incorrect file name Correct the filing record
